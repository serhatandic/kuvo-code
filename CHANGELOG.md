# Changelog

What changed in each release, for people using Kuvo Code. Add changes people would notice under
Unreleased, in plain words about what they'll see, as part of the change itself. Before a
release, Unreleased is renamed to the version; the release notes come from that section.

## Unreleased

### Highlights

- **Voice input**: click the microphone in the composer, or hold ⇧⌘Space, to talk instead of typing; your words are written on your Mac, and nothing is sent until you send it. <!-- symbol: mic.fill -->
- **Goals that keep going**: write `/goal all tests pass`, and the agent works turn after turn until a check says it's met. <!-- symbol: flag.checkered -->
- **What your agents made**: a bot's Shelf keeps its pages, pictures and memory, with every change on its Timeline. <!-- symbol: tray.full.fill -->
- **Answers you can try**: agents show charts, diagrams and things to click right in the conversation. <!-- symbol: chart.bar.fill -->
- **Settings that guide you**: pick folders, plugins and models from what's there instead of typing them. <!-- symbol: gearshape.fill; open: settings/worktrees -->

### All Changes

- Starting or stopping a computer's engine no longer waits minutes when an earlier start or stop there was cut short (by removing the computer, a crash, or the computer going to sleep): the next one goes ahead at once. One still under way is waited for, as before.
- In a group chat, a bot that has nothing to report on its part of the work now says nothing in the chat: its part shows as done, as before, but "PASS" no longer appears there as its report, nor in a notification that it finished its part. This holds for a bot on another of your computers too.
- In a group chat, the line under the bot that's talking now changes from one step to the next cleanly, and so does "… is thinking…" as the next bot takes its turn, also after one that had nothing to add: before, the old words and the new ones showed over each other for a moment as they changed.
- A session you stop while Auto is checking what it asked no longer shows as asking you later: in its bot's sessions bar its row kept saying "Asks to …" (and the Dock counted it) after the session went on, with nothing in it to answer.
- A Claude conversation's warning that a plan's limit is being used up now says how much of which limit is used ("You've used 25% of your weekly Claude limit."), and calls it close only from 90%. Claude Code warns early in a week too, and it said "You're close to your Claude usage limit" with a quarter of the week used, on your Mac and iPhone alike. Reaching the weekly limit now says so ("You've reached your weekly Claude limit."), rather than "your Claude usage limit for now".
- A card in the conversation opens and folds from its header: the header stays where it is, its words unfold and fold under it, and nothing above it moves. Before, the whole card slid in from below its place as it opened, its folded line and its words showed over each other, and a long name rewrapped as it went. This holds for messages to and from other agents, steps, a tool's details and wake-ups. Folded, a message's header is always one line: a long name is cut short with "…", leaving the rest of the line to how the message starts. A message you open at the end of the conversation opens down past the composer, as steps do, rather than taking the conversation up as it grows. Rest the pointer on a name that's cut short to read it whole.
- A machine whose sessions work day and night now takes a newer version of its coding agents from your Mac without waiting for a moment when nothing runs: they move to it once nothing has run there for half a minute (a group chat's discussion finishes first), and if that doesn't happen, new messages wait (kept, in their conversation's line) while the turns already running finish, for half an hour at most, at night after an hour, or after a day. No turn is cut and no message is lost or sent twice. Until then, Settings › Machines says so under the machine.
- A computer you remove from your machines, where the removal was cut short (it went to sleep, say, before Kuvo Code finished there), no longer starts Kuvo Code's background service again at its next restart: it stays off until a Mac adds it again.
- Your bots and sessions can start a session on another of your computers, as if they lived there: in a project there, on a model there, in their own mode and never with more approval than they have (Full Access only where you allowed it on that computer). They hear of it as they would here, list it and stop it, and a bot's Stop All reaches it. In that computer's sidebar, such a session says where it came from at the end of its row ("from Mini"), and who when you point at it. Each computer has a switch for whether your other computers may start work on it, in its Settings › Permissions: on for your machines, existing ones included, and off for the Mac with Kuvo Code until you turn it on. A computer that's asleep, off or offline is said so at once, and nothing is started there; one that goes quiet just as it takes the start says it may have started, and you hear if it did once it's back.
- With machines, going from the last computer in the sidebar to the first is one more step forward, like every other: its page comes in from the side the next one always does, and the names move on a step, as on your iPhone. Before, the pages slid back past every computer between (picking a name) or only faded in (a two-finger swipe). Going back from the first computer to the last is the same, the other way. This is the same in Settings' sidebar.
- Opening a step in a conversation at its end opens it under the pointer: what's above stays where it is, and the step opens down past the composer, as a disclosure row does. Before, the conversation jumped up to show all of it while it was still opening.
- A right-click menu stays as it opened while sessions work: in the sidebar, the transcript, the Work Queue and elsewhere, an open menu had its items made again each time a session started or finished a step, losing the item you pointed at or closing an open submenu. Each menu now shows what's true as you open it.
- A busy machine (one under a heavy load) is no longer said to be gone. Its conversations don't show "isn't connected" while it's only slow or its connection drops for a moment: that shows only once it's stayed out of reach for a few seconds and doesn't answer when checked. A message you send while its engine is slow waits for it, rather than coming back to the composer with "The engine isn't running.", and a reply it's slow to give is waited for rather than said to have timed out. If the engine stays too busy to answer, it says so in those words.
- Sessions a bot (or another agent that hears when they finish) started and leads no longer show as new to you: no blue dot on their row, their project, the computer's name or Recents, on your Mac and iPhone, since the bot hears of their work itself and its row shows it. One still shows as new when it has news for you, as its notification does: a reply after you wrote in it or answered what it asked, an error, or a notification its agent sent you. What it asks you still says Needs You. Those that had piled up are cleared as your computers update.
- **Mark All as Read** in the menu at the bottom of the sidebar reads everything new on all your computers at once, as Mail's does; right-click a computer's name over the sidebar to do it for that one. What asks you still says Needs You.
- In an empty composer, **⇧Tab** switches to the next mode, as in Claude Code: Ask First, Accept Edits, Auto, Full Access, then back to Ask First, as if you'd picked it from the Mode menu. Full Access is skipped until you've confirmed it once on the conversation's computer from the Mode menu. With words in the composer, **⇧Tab** moves back to the previous control, as before.
- Settings › Accounts no longer offers the engine's "Claude Agent" when you search for a provider to add: Claude comes through Claude Code, with the other coding agents. One you added before stays, as it was.
- A quote in a message of yours that's cut at four lines always has **Show All** under it. A quote of a few sentences was cut with no way to see the rest.
- With no model chosen (the composer says **Choose a Model**, like an old conversation whose model is gone), Send waits until you pick one: Return opens the model picker and your message stays in the field, instead of sending and an error coming back. A conversation on the engine's own OpenAI or xAI model says it needs a key for it, or a model under Codex or Grok for your ChatGPT or SuperGrok plan, instead of telling you to sign in to a plan you're already signed in to; its card's **Sign In** says it adds a key.
- A GitHub issue or pull request can be an item in the Work Queue: an agent adds one by its name (`gh:owner/repo#12`) or its address, or turns an item into the issue it's about. It shows with GitHub's mark and its name, like `kuvo-ai#12`, in place of a number, with the title GitHub has, and it's Done once it's closed or merged on GitHub (and back where it was if it's reopened there). Its name in a message is a chip, as `#243` is, and opens it; the issue's name in the item opens it on GitHub. Kuvo Code reads it with GitHub's `gh` on the computer the queue is on, only while it's on screen, and never changes anything on GitHub.
- A conversation you open from another, like a session from the bot that started it, or a bot's conversation from a group chat, now opens in its place with the way back at its top, as a subagent's does. **‹** and the name of where you came from (or **View › Back**, ⌘[) takes you back there, where you were reading, one step at a time. ⌘-click an **Open**, or right-click it and choose **Open in New Tab**, to open it in a tab of its own instead. A conversation you pick in the sidebar starts fresh.
- The usage panel shows each Claude account once, however many of your computers are signed in to it: a Mac's Claude now says which account it is once it has had a turn there, so it joins the same account on your other computers instead of showing again as "Claude · mini-local". Where this Mac has no Claude account of its own yet, your other computers' Claude accounts take Claude's bar, with its spend under the first, rather than a second "Claude" with the computers' names after it. A second account keeps its own name ("Claude · Athas").
- The Work Queue is easier to read. Each row starts with its number, with whose it is and when it last changed on the same small line, then its title with the row's whole width, and what the agent says it's doing while it works; Next has no 1-2-3 any more, its order is the list's. The mark of an item in progress turns, in In progress blue. With the pointer on a row, **Open Session** takes the place of its time; it's also first when you right-click a row, with **Open Session in New Tab**. An item's page reads top to bottom: its number and status, its title, a line with where it's kept, whose queue it's in and when it was added, a card with the session on it, what it's doing and **Open Session**, what it says, then its activity, oldest first (who added it, notes, hand-offs). An item a session is at work on says **In progress** only, not its category too. And a session a bot or another session put on an item now lists that item in its own Work Queue, first, under **Working on**, saying whose it is: open it from there, as its owner's to change.
- The Work Queue's numbers add up: **All** counts every item, Done ones too, and lists Done last, folded, so In progress and each category add up to it; the header and the tab say how many are still open ("24 open"). Drag an item to put it in order: a line shows where it will land, above or below the row you're over, and dropped on a row in another category, or on a category's name, it moves there. The order is kept on its computer, so your iPhone and your agents see it too. A machine that's off no longer leaves its old list in the panel as if it were live: the panel shows what your Mac last heard from it, says so, and shows nothing on it as in progress.
- An item's Activity in the Work Queue now says what happened to it as well as its notes: when an agent started and stopped work on it, when you or an agent moved it (to another category, or to the top of its own) and when it was done, each with who and when, oldest first. It goes with the item to another computer. Items from before keep their notes and show these from now on.
- Auto rejects far less safe work. A session at work in a project's folder edits that project's git worktrees, wherever they are (a throwaway copy in `/tmp`, say), as its own folder: its file tools there aren't reviewed or asked about. Auto's reviewer is told where the session works and, for one another agent started, the task it was given, so it no longer holds back work in the very folder the task named. Once it allows the agent to change a file in a folder, its changes there go ahead without asking it again for the rest of the conversation; that folder shows in Auto's Decisions, where **Revoke** has it check each change there again, and leaving Auto takes them all back. A long test file of the project's own is read in part, with how it differs from its last commit, instead of being held back for its length. A session in a project's git worktree made elsewhere is listed under its project. And an agent that starts a session in a project with a folder of it (or of one of its worktrees) gets it working in that folder, where before the folder was quietly dropped.
- A conversation has no gray pills any more. When a bot's or a lead's sessions finish or ask something, each shows as a message from that session, with what it said and **Open**. What changed since the reply before, like another model or effort, Plan mode or a summary, shows beside the next reply's time. Notes written only for the agent, like "Background command finished" or the engine's "User requested that active blocking work be moved to the background" (which no one requested), no longer show; Kuvo Code's notes for you, like a hook that failed, are a quiet line. Who allowed a step is said on the step: "Allowed by you", "Allowed by Auto-review", or "Not allowed by" the bot or session that answered, with why.
- A session's row in the sidebar shows its pull request: green while it's open, purple once merged, gray as a draft or closed, and red all over when a check fails. A session with a branch of its own and no pull request shows its branch, and a session in the project's own folder looks as before. Rest the pointer on a row and a card opens beside it with how it stands and since when, its branch, its model, effort and account, and the pull request with its checks and size. Kuvo Code reads pull requests with GitHub's `gh` on the computer the session is on, at most once a minute and only while their rows are on screen. A pull request a session made, with Create Pull Request or its own `gh pr create`, is that session's.
- While you write a message, the bars over the composer (sessions at work, wake-ups, a goal) fold into their one line, so the conversation keeps its room; they come back as they were once it's sent or cleared, and a click on the line brings them back meanwhile. The small double chevron just over the card's top edge folds them all into that line again, without taking a row of the card, while each bar's own chevron still folds just that bar.
- Pictures an agent shows appear as pictures again in a session on another of your computers, where they were file cards. Several shown one after another sit side by side in a row, with their captions. Every picture opens the same way, an agent's, your message's, the composer's: click one to look closer, zoom with a pinch, a double-click, ⌘+ ⌘− ⌘0 or the zoom menu, save it, and go to the conversation's other pictures, yours and the agent's from every message, with a two-finger swipe on the trackpad, ← →, or the ‹ › that show as you move the pointer. A picture in the side panel fits the panel at first and zooms the same way, rather than opening at its full size, cropped, and one of the conversation's pictures there swipes to the others too.
- On a busy Mac, an agent's request to run a command outside the sandbox (`sandbox_ask`), or another request to Kuvo Code's background service, no longer fails now and then before it reaches you: the service could close the connection the request was sent on just as it went. It now keeps an unused connection open well past the time it tells the app, so the app is the one to close it.
- A machine now sets up its own browser for agents: it downloads Chrome for Testing from Google itself (on Linux, with the libraries and fonts it needs from Rocky Linux), checks every file against the checksum this version of Kuvo Code pins before using it, and keeps it in `~/.kuvo-code`. Your Mac no longer downloads it and sends it over, so a machine you reach only from your iPhone has a browser too, and a new version comes with Kuvo Code's updates. The machine needs to reach storage.googleapis.com (and dl.rockylinux.org on Linux) over HTTPS; if it can't, the browser's page in the panel says so and it tries again by itself.
- A permission's answers are numbered in the composer's place, as on your iPhone and in Claude Code and Codex: **1** Allow Once, **2** Always Allow…, **3** Don't Allow, **4** Tell It What to Do Instead…, which opens a line to say no with your words. Press the number to answer; **Return** still allows once, and **Esc** still goes to the line. Once the ask in view is answered, the next one after it shows, and what you wrote under an ask stays with it while you look at the others.
- A session an agent starts in a new worktree, or in one of a project's other folders (or a folder inside the project), shows under that project in the sidebar from the moment it appears, on your Mac and your iPhone, rather than as a project named after its folder. Sessions already in a project's other folders move under it too, and Remove Project archives them with the rest. Opening one of those folders yourself still makes it a project of its own.
- Every icon in the composer's row is one size and weight, the size of the words beside it, and sits on their line: the paperclip, the mode's icon (with its name or alone), the chevrons by the mode and the model, the microphone, and Send and Stop. The paperclip and the microphone were bigger than the rest.
- In a short window, the lists that open over the composer as you type `/`, `@` or `&` stay in the window: they're as tall as the room over the composer, and scroll inside past that, the row you choose with ↑ and ↓ always in sight. Before, a long list ran off the top of the window.
- Your answer to a question Codex asked reaches it when Codex had turned it away for a moment just as it started to wait. Before, it could miss the wait that began then, and Codex slept on for hours as if you hadn't answered.
- With Reduce Motion on, opening the side panel while you work in the session beside the conversation keeps you there: the keyboard stays in that session (or in the panel's terminal you click into), and the panel stays that session's. Before, the conversation took the keyboard back, and the panel switched to the conversation's, without the terminal you'd just opened.
- A command the sandbox stopped, which the agent asks to run outside it, has its answers numbered too (Allow Once, Always Allow…, Don't Allow), and their number keys pick them, as every other ask's.
- What Auto's reviewer is still checking never takes the composer's place or tells you, on this Mac or your machines, however long the check takes; one it leaves for you comes as soon as its computer says so.
- In Ask First and Accept Edits, an agent's own tools read what its commands saved in their temporary folder (a screenshot, a log) and the files you sent in the conversation without asking you, as they read its project. In Auto, a command run outside the sandbox may name the files you sent in that conversation (Auto's reviewer judges it as any other); another conversation's stay refused.
- `rm -rf $TMPDIR` in a command no longer fails with "Operation not permitted": the command's temporary folder can be removed and made again, only never as a link.
- Kuvo Code's background work no longer waits on macOS while it asks you about a folder: a project on your Desktop or in Documents, or your SSH or GitHub settings linked into one (from a dotfiles folder, say), is looked at off the box's thread, so only what needs your answer waits for it, and the rest of Kuvo Code keeps answering. Until macOS has your answer, commands in the sandbox can't read `~/.ssh` or gh's sign-in where those lead into such a folder.
- Asking macOS to start Kuvo Code's background work, and asking the box what the engine starts with, never hold up the app, and are heard as they end even when something they started keeps running.
- A bot no longer hears a session's report twice, once as a message and again as "[Session finished]". A session that a bot or another agent started, and whose finish it hears, is now told that its last reply is its report and reaches them when it finishes, so it doesn't also send it as a message. Messages stay for questions and progress along the way.
- On a busy computer, a bot or lead hears "[Session finished]" once for each finish, where it could get the same report five times or more. The finish could be looked at several times at once while the computer was slow to read the session's messages, and each time told again; and a message whose delivery seemed to fail but had arrived was sent again as a new one. A session that's told something new and finishes again is still told again.
- A notification for your iPhone from one of your machines is no longer lost when the machine's connection to the relay is closing just as it's sent: it goes once the connection is back, as it already did from your Mac.
- An agent importing an old backlog into the Work Queue in several parts on a computer that gets its numbers from another keeps every item's own number; before, a part could renumber items the part before it had claimed. Only an item whose number another item already has gets a new one, and the import says which, and to what. Importing the same backlog again gives items an earlier import renumbered their own numbers back, without making them twice.
- The Work Queue is one list across all your computers: **All Conversations** shows the items on your Mac and every machine, from any of them, each saying which computer it's on (as that computer last told it while it's asleep), and a bot's own list also has the items of the sessions it started on other computers. What you do to one (move it, delete it, Undo) is done on its computer. An agent can hand an item to a session or bot on another computer, and it moves there once. Every computer now takes its item numbers from the first computer you set up.
- Agents keep your work items in a Work Queue: when you ask for something to be done later, the agent adds it there instead of a notes file, numbered #1, #2…, with its description, pictures, notes, progress and the sessions working on it. Each conversation has its own, and a bot also sees the items of the sessions it started. See it in the side panel's new **Work Queue** tab (⌥⌘U): In progress with what's being done, Next in order, the rest as counts, and, with the panel expanded, filter chips over the list and the item beside it. Drag items to reorder them or move them to another category, add your own categories, and delete them, with Undo and Recently Deleted to bring one back. **All Conversations** shows every item on all your computers at once, each saying which computer it's on. When you or an agent write an item's number, like #243, it shows as a chip with its title and where it stands; click it to open the item.
- The subagents you make in Settings › Agents work in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Grok and Antigravity sessions too, with their instructions, on every account. In Claude Code they win over one of the same name in `~/.claude/agents`, and keep to what Settings › Agents lets them do. Before, only Kuvo Code's own agent had them.
- **Subagents use** in Settings › Agents picks one model for the parts of a task an agent hands off, from any of your accounts, on each computer; a subagent with a model of its own keeps it. Each coding agent takes it when it's one of its models.
- With Background Work on, your Mac is back within reach of your iPhone and your other computers as soon as it's restarted, before you open Kuvo Code, and with it closed: agents on your machines can write to it and reach its sessions again, and your iPhone connects. Before, it stayed out of reach until you opened Kuvo Code. Turning the phone link off, or removing your last machine, still keeps it off.
- Your iPhone shows all of a Mac's controls (folder pickers, Autopilot, the approval default, search) when it connects while Kuvo Code on the Mac is closed, and its Chats are where the Mac keeps them. Before, those controls were missing until Kuvo Code was opened there.
- On your Mac, with Kuvo Code open, the sandbox's state in Settings and an agent's request to run a command outside it (`sandbox_ask`) work, where they could fail before, and a notification an agent sends you (`notify_user`) shows on the Mac as well as your iPhone. A finished run's and group chat's notifications on the Mac are worded as on your iPhone, in one sentence.
- Picking a coding agent's model (Claude Code, Codex and others) in a conversation on your Mac works from your iPhone also when Kuvo Code on the Mac is closed or not answering, as it does on your machines. Before, it could fail with "Kuvo Code on this Mac isn't answering", also while it was open. A move that fails partway no longer leaves an extra copy of the conversation behind: it stays as it was, with what you'd left waiting, so you can try again. If Kuvo Code on the Mac has the conversation open, the new one takes its tab there, with what you were writing. The new conversation is also told about your project's other folders.
- With Kuvo Code closed, an agent whose command the sandbox stopped can still ask you to run it outside (`sandbox_ask`), and your iPhone can read and switch a conversation's Autopilot on your Mac. Before, the agent was told the browser needs Kuvo Code open, and Autopilot showed as off.
- With Background Work on, a hiccup in how the open app talks to its background service no longer cuts off what only the app answers: your iPhone's requests for the Mac's own pages (its usage, machines and the like) and your keys in the Keychain stay reachable. Before, they could stop answering, with "Kuvo Code on this Mac isn't answering", until you quit and opened Kuvo Code again.
- A Claude Code subagent sent off to work in the background (as Claude Code does unless told otherwise, a reviewer say) keeps working after the turn that started it ends: its tools run, ask you or are refused by your rules as the conversation's own would be. Before, every tool it used once that turn ended was refused with "There's no turn under way for this", so its review or task quietly didn't happen.
- Each row of the bar over a bot's composer that lists its sessions says which account the session runs on, between its model and its project: "Opus 5.5 · high · Claude · Work · Kuvo Code", or "Copilot" for your GitHub Copilot plan, named as the usage panel names it, with the name you gave a coding agent's account you added in Settings › Accounts. Where the row is short, the account gives way first, then the project; the session's title always keeps its room. On your iPhone too.
- A bot's list of its sessions, and the bot's work bar in the app, no longer give a session you or it stopped (or one that failed) the reply it wrote in an earlier turn as its last reply, so the bot doesn't take that earlier answer for how the stopped turn ended. What the session said in the turn that was stopped still shows. A session stopped while it was at rest between turns shows as stopped too, instead of as finished: the bot's list gives the reply from its last turn as written before the stop, and the work bar shows none.
- A bot that just moved to another computer reaches the sessions it left behind at once. Before, for up to a minute after the move, its list of sessions said the computer it left "isn't answering", and a stop or an answer it gave was only kept for later, though that computer was awake.
- A message a bot sends right after it moved to another computer, to a conversation on the computer it left, comes from the bot, by its name, and an answer it waits for goes back to it. Before, for a short while after the move, it read as from "an agent on" the other computer, and its answer couldn't go back.
- Custom Auto names each model as the model picker does: its notices and what one model hands the next say "Claude Code (Opus 5.5, high)" rather than "Claude Code (default, high)". A list that holds a Claude Code model both by its name and as Claude Code's default shows it once, and runs it once; an entry you saved as the default goes on following whichever model Claude Code recommends, also after you edit the list. A bot on Claude Code's default that starts a session on a model that just failed is again told which model it can use instead: the one it runs on.
- A video or another big file you send from your iPhone, or share to a conversation from another app, gets there on a slow connection too. Before, the iPhone could give up while it was still on its way and say the computer "didn't answer": the file reached the computer, but the message with it never reached the conversation. A message that reached your computer whole now always goes on to its conversation.
- A session waiting on a test run or another command it left running in the background no longer waits for ever when that command stops early. If the agent's process ends between turns (killed from outside, or crashed), or Kuvo Code restarts (an update, say), the command stops with it: the session is now woken, told what stopped (and what it printed, or where, when that's known), and can start it again. Before, it sat idle, shown as finished, waiting for a word that never came. Commands Codex and Grok leave running also keep going after their turn ends, and their sessions show as working meanwhile; before, they were stopped 15 minutes after the turn.
- The side panel stays open or closed in each conversation as you left it there: closing it in one no longer closes it in all the others, and opening it in one no longer opens it in a bot's or session's where you'd closed it. A conversation you haven't been in yet starts as the one you were in, and an agent showing you a file opens the panel only in its own conversation. The agent's browser keeps working with the panel hidden. The sessions list on the left is still one for the whole window.
- A GitHub Copilot account's Claude models work as soon as you sign in, sign in again or sign out, on this Mac and on your machines, with nothing restarted and no session cut short (from the engine's first start after this update). Before, Kuvo Code learned what they need only while its own agent was at work, and it took effect at the engine's next start, so they failed with "Extra inputs are not permitted" until then.
- **Refresh Models**, in the **…** menu of the account in use in Settings › Accounts (on your iPhone too), brings models your plan gained (GitHub Copilot, GitLab Duo, OpenCode and others whose models come from your account) into the model picker without a restart. A project with work going on gets them once it's done.
- A picture an agent changes and shows you again shows the change, not the picture it showed the first time. A file an agent on a machine shows is no longer drawn from a file at the same path on this Mac.
- A bot on Claude Code saves its memory again on a Mac. Since the sandbox came in, every change it made to its `MEMORY.md` there was refused with "Operation not permitted", so it kept notes elsewhere or not at all. Bots on Codex, Grok, Cursor and Kuvo Code's own engine weren't affected.
- Your computers' names over the sidebar take less room, as on your iPhone: the one in view first, larger and bold, then your other computers faded beside it, and "+3" for those that don't fit. Picking one slides it to the front. A long name is cut at its end rather than in the middle. Settings' sidebar shows them the same way.
- A bot or chat you move to another computer while Kuvo Code is open works there at once: what you send it goes to that computer, and it opens on that computer's page in the sidebar. ## 0.1.0

Kuvo Code's first public release: a native Mac harness for coding agents that works with any model, on the opencode engine.

### Highlights

- **Any model.** Sign in with a subscription you already have, add an API key, or start with free models that need no account. Pick the model and effort for each message.
- **Built for the Mac.** SwiftUI and Liquid Glass on macOS 26. Sessions sit under their project in the sidebar, transcripts update live, and edits and commands open to show what they did.
- **Review changes like a pull request.** The Changes tab shows the project's uncommitted changes from git, with unchanged lines folded and pictures shown before and after. Comment on any line, and your comments go to the agent with your next message.
- **Files, terminals and a browser.** The side panel holds them as tabs: the project's files with code highlighted and Markdown rendered, your shell in the project folder, and a web browser. A new tab offers the tools and the sites you visit most, and Settings › Panel sets it up the way you like.
- **Browsing for the agent, with any model.** The agent can open pages in the panel's browser, read them, click, type and scroll, and you see every step.
- **Attach anything.** Drop, paste or pick files of any kind. Pictures show up in the transcript.
- **Plans and spend in the menu bar.** One bar per provider shows the limit closest to running out; click it for pace, reset times and spend for today and the last 30 days. Covers Claude, Codex, Copilot, Cursor, OpenCode, OpenRouter, Z.ai and more.
- **Build and Plan modes.** Plan mode talks a change through before making it. With **Ask First**, the agent asks before it edits files or runs commands.
- **Updates itself.** Kuvo Code checks for signed updates once a day and shows what's new before it installs one. **Kuvo Code › Check for Updates…** checks right away.
- **Kept apart from your own opencode.** The engine is the official opencode, unmodified, with its sessions, accounts and settings in Kuvo Code's own folder.
